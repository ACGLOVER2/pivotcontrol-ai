# Case Study: [SC] / the federal systems integrator

**Seller:** [SC], Solutions Consultant, the software vendor  
**Account:** a federal systems integrator  
**Call type:** SC Calibration / Technical Discovery  
**Call duration:** ~30 minutes  
**Final PCI:** 47/100, Developing  

---

## Overview

This audit covers [SC]'s performance during a technical calibration session with the end customer representatives [Inspection Lead] and [Inspection Analyst]. [SC]'s objective was to understand the document validation workflow well enough to build a targeted demo. [AD] (AD) was present to open with account context and facilitate, but [SC] was the primary driver.

---

## The Call

The primary pressure window came at approximately 11:05, when [Inspection Lead] said the inspection team needs to stay in SAP, and [SC] responded:

> *"I'm struggling here, where we're going to be able to assist. I'm still getting a little bit lost."*

Spoken directly to the client. This is the defining moment of the audit.

[SC]'s best moment was his platform value summary at 20:25:

> *"We can take any document, classify them, extract key data, run business rules to verify against SAP, and display it all on a single screen."*

Five steps. One sentence. No hedging. That is executive-level framing.

The gap between those two moments is the entire development opportunity.

---

## Key Finding: The SC's Primary Failure Mode

When [SC] knew the answer, he communicated with authority. When the path was unclear, his structure collapsed outward, he verbalized his confusion rather than converting it into a diagnostic question.

The correct behavior when fit is ambiguous:

> *"Before I map our solution to this, let me make sure I have the right picture. Walk me through what the inspector sees on screen the moment the document arrives."*

This converts internal confusion into a structured diagnostic, which signals curiosity and control rather than uncertainty.

**The pattern detected:**
- Confusion verbalized within 90 seconds of the pressure onset
- Sentence length increased from average 20 words to 40+ words under pressure
- Authority language shifted: "I know" → "I guess" and "I'm not sure"
- Discovery questions stopped, [SC] moved to explaining before fully understanding

---

## Why This Case Study [Stakeholder]ers for the SC Rubric

The [SC] audit produced the clearest articulation of the SC's primary failure mode in the beta cycle. It is not a confidence problem. It is a structural problem: the instinct to externalize confusion rather than convert it into a diagnostic question.

This finding is now a core element of the SC rubric and the SC-specific coaching curriculum.

**The SC development principle that emerged:**  
*Never broadcast uncertainty to a client. Convert confusion into a question.*

---

## Strengths to Protect

Despite the Developing score, [SC] had three genuinely strong moments:
- Platform value summary at 20:25, five steps, one sentence, no hedging
- Technical objection handling at 25:05 to 26:07, on-prem vs cloud vs non US data residency, all answered clean
- Peer reference to large financial institutions to neutralize the data residency concern, strong instinct

The PCI of 47 reflects the confusion window's impact on authority stability. The capability is present. The development work is specific.
